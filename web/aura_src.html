The wave

Cortical spreading depolarization moves at about 3 mm per minute (Lashley timed his own aura in 1941; Hadjikhani and colleagues imaged it in 2001). The front here moves at exactly that speed across the flattened cortex.

Why it speeds up

The cortex maps the field by w = 17.3·log((z + 0.75°)/0.75°) mm (Horton & Hoyt 1991). Constant speed on the cortex becomes ever faster in the periphery, so the crescent widens as it drifts out.

Why zigzags

Each patch of cortex prefers one edge orientation, arranged in pinwheels about 1.4 mm apart (drawn three times wider here, so a column covers more than a pixel). Unclamped, each patch shows the atom it codes for. The 64 atoms are the ones Model 6 learned from natural photographs.

What this is not

A model built to make the idea visible, not a medical description. Real auras vary widely. The shimmer is deliberately slow (well under three changes a second) and stops under reduced-motion settings.
